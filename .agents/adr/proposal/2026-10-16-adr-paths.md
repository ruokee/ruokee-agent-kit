# ADR paths

Draft owner: Ruokee
Draft writer: OMP

English | [中文](./2026-10-16-adr-paths.zh.md)

## Motivation

Keep an ADR decision's canonical path stable across reversals.

Successive decisions currently use different dates and names. The ADR mechanism alone has records dated 2026-08-22, 2026-09-02, 2026-09-23, and 2026-10-15. Moving the current path at every reversal makes current references move with it. Dates alone also cannot distinguish successive archives of the same decision on one day.

The flat lifecycle directories become harder to browse as records accumulate. Some historical titles and filenames also carry details that belong in the body under the [documentation naming rules](../../rule/documentation.md#naming). Adding a revision suffix to an already long name would retain that naming problem.

## Proposal

### Identity

Use a decision's first proposal date and a short canonical topic name to identify its current record and all its archived versions. An ordinary reversal preserves that date and name. The actual archival date remains in `Archived: YYYY-MM-DD`; dated `Changes` entries retain their event dates.

A successor that carries on the original topic retains its identity and revision sequence. A topic extracted into an independent decision gets its own first proposal date, canonical name, and sequence. Preserve the actual source and reversal relationships. A shared predecessor or a `Reverses` link does not by itself make two records the same decision.

A proposal uses its own first proposal date, including when it proposes to reverse an older decision. Rejection preserves that proposal date. The reversal proposal's date does not replace the older decision's identity date.

### Paths

Keep `.agents/adr` and its four lifecycle directories. Place ADR bodies under their identity year and month:

```text
.agents/adr/proposal/YYYY/MM/DD-name.md
.agents/adr/decision/YYYY/MM/DD-name.md
.agents/adr/rejected/YYYY/MM/DD-name.md
.agents/adr/archived/YYYY/MM/DD-rN-name.md
```

`YYYY` is a four-digit year, and `MM` and `DD` are two-digit month and day values. The Chinese counterpart uses the same directory and basename with `.zh.md`. Both language files share their date and revision number and move together.

Only archived decisions carry `rN`. The first reversal produces `r1`, and each later reversal of that same decision increments `N` once. The sequence continues across days, months, and years. Another decision's reversals, ordinary updates, naming corrections, directory moves, language counterparts, and multiple relationship links do not consume numbers in that sequence.

The current decision and every archive use the same first proposal date for `YYYY/MM/DD`, even when archival happens in a later year. This groups a decision's history together; it does not impose a file-count limit on a month directory. Keep the existing `.gitkeep` in each lifecycle root and create time directories only for actual records. The ADR guide and glossary stay at the ADR root.

### Reversal history

Keep the complete successor at the canonical `decision/` path and move the replaced bilingual version to its next `archived/` revision. The successor's `Reverses` points to that archive, and the archive's `Reversed by` points to its actual successor.

When version A is reversed by B, A's archive initially links to B at the current path. If C later reverses B, that same path contains C. A's `Reversed by` must then follow B to B's archive rather than name C as A's direct successor. B keeps its link to A, and C links to B's archive.

References to current authority continue to use the canonical current path. References to a particular historical choice follow that version to its archive. Classify links by what they cite, not by whether their source file is current or archived. Recompute relative paths from each source's new directory, repair reciprocal language links and affected inbound links, and preserve or update referenced heading fragments. Stable file paths do not make heading fragments immutable.

### Naming

Apply the [documentation rules](../../rule/documentation.md#naming) and [well-said](../../../skills/well-said/SKILL.md) to every retained ADR title, subsection heading, and filename. Use a short imperative, phrase, or noun naming one subject, and leave the details in the body. A date prefix in a `Changes` heading does not exempt its topic text from these rules.

The complete leaf filename must not exceed 60 characters, including `DD`, any `rN`, the topic, the language suffix, and the extension. Moving the year and month into directories does not replace name cleanup or relax this limit. Once names are corrected, ordinary reversals keep the canonical path; erroneous historical names do not become permanent aliases.

### Historical correction

Correct all retained proposals, current decisions, archived decisions, and rejected proposals, not just future records. Determine each record's identity, first proposal date, actual reversal order, canonical topic, and language pair from its content, relationship fields, proposal sources, and relevant Git history. Correct evidenced relationship errors rather than treating existing names or links as authoritative ordering.

Preserve every recorded choice, reason, constraint, uncertainty, historical capability scope, original author, actual archival date, and event date. Name and directory corrections do not reverse the choices recorded in those ADRs or add a reversal to each identity. The original paths and per-record filename dates remain recoverable from Git history.

Repair affected relationship links, body links, language links, inbound references, and fragments in the same change. Do not retain duplicate old ADR files to conceal missing reference repairs. A historical format example or Git object path may retain its original spelling when that spelling is the fact being cited.

Synchronize the ADR guide, glossary, documentation rules, repository specification, and affected repository references in both languages. The guide owns the detailed format; the repository specification states the lasting goals and observable invariants. The existing ADR maintenance Skill proposal keeps its separate choice and lifecycle.

### Mechanism contract

Reverse [Clarify the ADR content boundaries](../decision/2026-10-15-clarify-adr-content-boundaries.md) for its date-preservation and maintenance boundary. Its effective maintenance clause requires naming edits to preserve every recorded date. The [ADR guide](../README.md#document-layout) expresses dates in individual flat filenames. Existing mechanism successors use different filename dates, while this proposal requires one first proposal date for the entire identity. Keeping each successor's filename date and normalizing them all to the identity's first date cannot both hold. This is a change to date meaning and the permitted historical correction, not a claim that a rename alone warrants reversal.

The complete mechanism successor must retain all other still-effective rules, including:

- ADR terminology, the small paired glossary, the two document types, directory-expressed lifecycle state, and the absence of a `Status` field.
- Search and classification before a new requirement, one proposal unless the maintainer requests a split, an approved proposal's independent merge before implementation, and removal of the consumed proposal with its resulting decisions.
- Same-directory bilingual authority, same-change completion, reciprocal links, semantic-conflict handling, owner and original-writer metadata, required body sections, and the optional analysis section.
- Self-contained motivation, necessary contract boundaries, preservation of relocated promises and historical detail, real alternatives, credible risks, and the accepted empty-section forms.
- Non-conflicting updates under dated `Changes`, named incompatibility before reversal, complete successors preserving still-effective rules, actual archival metadata, and accurate direct relationships.
- Maintainer control of approval, rejection, and archival; factual maintenance without conflicting choices; and freezing after the applicable relationship and factual link repairs. Naming maintenance continues to preserve historical substance, with the identity-date normalization defined here.
- Review, repository search, and Git history as the maintenance approach. This proposal adds no dedicated Skill, classification system, translation sidecar, archive manifest, index, or permanent maintenance script. The existing condition for adding a mechanical check after repeated maintenance or an observed failure remains unchanged.

## Alternatives considered

**Keep the existing per-record dates and flat layout.** This preserves current paths only until the next reversal. It leaves current references moving, same-day archives needing another distinction, and lifecycle roots accumulating records without year and month grouping.

**Apply the conventions only to future records.** This leaves existing long names, inconsistent identity dates, and incorrect historical relationships outside the correction. Readers would still need two naming conventions to follow one decision's history.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Every retained ADR body uses its lifecycle's year and month layout, with a complete, semantically aligned same-directory language pair. Current decisions and proposals have no revision suffix; archived decisions use `rN`.
2. After historical normalization, a decision's current path and first proposal date remain unchanged through ordinary reversals on the same day or across days, months, and years. Every replaced version remains separately accessible.
3. Each identity's first reversal creates `r1`, later reversals increment its own sequence, and unrelated decisions or ordinary maintenance do not alter that sequence.
4. Current records and their archives share the first proposal date for directory placement. `Archived` retains the actual archival date, and `Changes` entries retain their actual event dates.
5. A continuing original topic retains its path and sequence; an extracted independent topic gets a distinct path and its own sequence without losing its true source or reversal relationships.
6. `Reverses` and `Reversed by` identify actual direct predecessors and successors after repeated reversals. Current-authority references and historical-version references resolve to their intended content, including heading fragments and reciprocal language links.
7. All retained ADR titles, subsection headings, and filenames satisfy the short, single-subject naming rules. Complete filenames, including revision and Chinese suffix where applicable, satisfy the 60-character limit.
8. Historical correction preserves the recorded choices, reasons, constraints, uncertainties, capability scope, authorship, archival dates, and event dates. Pure normalization does not create additional reversals or leave duplicate compatibility files.
9. The complete ADR mechanism successor preserves the unaffected rules listed above. The guide, glossary, documentation rules, repository specification, and actual files express the same contract in both languages.

## Risks

Mistaking shared ancestry for shared identity could combine independent decisions or assign archives the wrong sequence, making historical choices appear to supersede one another when they did not.

A path migration or later reversal that treats every reference as current authority could redirect a historical citation to different content. Missing a reciprocal link or heading fragment can also leave part of a language pair or its cited evidence unreachable.
